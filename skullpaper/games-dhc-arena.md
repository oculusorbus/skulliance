# DHC Arena

**Three of your Fighters against three of theirs, on one shared board.**

DHC Arena is where the Fighters you assemble in [[games-dhc-fighters]] actually
fight. You pick a Crew of three, pick a rival, and the two Crews trade turns on
a single 7x7 board. Every gem you match belongs to somebody — match it and that
Fighter acts.

Win and a **wildcard trait** drops into your collection, which is the loop: play
the other games to earn traits, assemble Fighters out of them, send those
Fighters into the Arena, and win more traits.

The art is Maxingo's, the same trait set the assembler draws from. The Fighters
on the board are your saved assemblies, layer for layer.

---

## Getting in

| | |
|---|---|
| **Crew size** | 3 Fighters |
| **Battles a day** | 6 |
| **Recovery after any battle** | 4 hours |
| **Extra recovery for a Fighter who fell** | 4 to 12 hours, depending on how deep your roster is |
| **Trait drops** | 1 per win, sharing DHC Fighters' cap of 3 a day |

You need **three Fighters standing** to enter. A Fighter who has just fought is
**recovering** for four hours whatever the result, and one who was knocked out
recovers longer — from four extra hours if you only own three Fighters, up to
twelve if you own plenty. A deep roster rests harder because it has somewhere
else to turn; a player with exactly three is not locked out of the game for a
day.

Your Crew list pages once you have more than twelve Fighters, and **picks
survive paging** — the three you have chosen stay listed above the grid in
formation order, and clicking one there drops it.

**One battle at a time.** You cannot start another while one is still going —
walking away from a battle you were losing would otherwise skip recovery
entirely, which is the only real cost in the game. A battle nobody has touched
for **six hours** is forfeited: it resolves as a defeat, with the recovery and
the record a defeat carries, so a dropped connection can never lock you out of
the Arena for good.

**Only the attacker's Fighters need to recover.** Your Crew defends around the clock
whether you are logged in or not, and defending costs them nothing — a defender
never chose to be there, so being popular must never be something you can be
punished for.

## The board

Five kinds of gem on a 7x7 board:

| Gem | What it is |
|---|---|
| 🔴 red | your **front** Fighter |
| 🟠 amber | your **mid** Fighter |
| 🟣 violet | your **back** Fighter |
| 🔵 blue | 🛡️ **Shield** — armour for your whole Crew |
| 🟢 green | ⚡ **Charge** — builds towards an eruption |

Both Crews share these five. The colour is the **rank**, not the person, so the
same violet gem drives your back Fighter on your turn and theirs on theirs. Leave
violet lying around and their back rank is the one who gets to use it.

**A move is a slide, not a swap.** Pick a gem and drag it any distance along its
row or column; everything between shifts one step back toward where it came
from. It is the same motion as [[games-monstrocity]] and [[games-skull-swap]], so
it should already be in your hands. A slide that makes no match is free — it
reverts and costs you nothing.

### Match size is reach

Their Crew stands in a line, and how far into it you can hit is decided by how
big your match was:

| Match | Reaches |
|---|---|
| 3 | their front rank |
| 4 | their mid rank |
| 5 or more | their back rank |

Reach is a ceiling, not a requirement — you can always hit something shallower,
and if nothing shallow enough is left standing the frontmost survivor takes it.
This is what makes the formation a real decision: **the order you pick your Crew
sets front, mid and back**, so the Fighter you least want reached goes last.

### Cascades, chains and multi-matches

- A **cascade** — gems falling into a new match on their own — multiplies the
  damage of each further link.
- A **multi-match** is two or more matches from the one slide. Six gems across
  the matches pays **+30% damage**; nine or more pays **+90%**. Cascades are
  luck, but two matches from one slide is something you found, so only the
  slide itself is paid this way.
- A match of **5 or more gives you another turn**.

### Bombs

- A match of **4** arms a ✛ bomb, which clears its whole row and column.
- A match of **5 or more** arms a 💣 board bomb, which clears everything.

A bomb sits on the board wearing a colour, and **either side can set it off** by
matching that colour. It is not yours because you made it. Anything you arm goes
live at the end of your move, never inside your own cascade — so a bomb is
always a risk you are leaving on the table, not a free extra hit.

When a battle is decided, **every bomb still on the board goes off** as a last
hurrah. Nothing is resolved by it; it is the payoff for a board littered with
bombs nobody dared spend.

## Fighters

Every Fighter's numbers come out of the traits you gave it, and a **weapon
decides how it fights**:

| | Weapon style | Does |
|---|---|---|
| 🔨 | Smash | one big hit |
| 🪓 | Cleave | hits a whole rank |
| 🩸 | Drain | hits and steals health |
| ⛏️ | Break | smashes shields |
| 🎯 | Snipe | often hits harder |
| 🏹 | Volley | a weak hit to everything |
| 🗡️ | Bleed | keeps hurting for 3 rounds |
| ⚔️ | Double | strikes twice |

Torso sets health, weapon sets power and style, headgear sets how often a hit
lands big. **Rarity helps but does not decide** — the spread between two traits
of the same tier is deliberately wider than the gap between the tiers
themselves, so a well-matched common Crew genuinely beats a legendary one more
often than not. A Crew of commons wins about seven battles in ten against a Crew
of legendaries in simulation, and that is the intended answer: the Arena is
about how you play, not what you were lucky enough to draw.

**Charge** builds one point for the whole Crew on every ⚡ match and erupts at
**10**, hitting everything on the other side at once. The meter sits beside your
Crew's name, so you can always see how close it is.

**The arena itself is the defender's front-rank background**, and it does
something: Fractured Signal means everyone lands big hits more often,
Overclocked is 10% more damage all round, Dense Cover makes Shield 50% stronger,
Low Gravity builds Charge twice as fast, Corrosive Haze gives everyone 8% less
health. It is shown under the board every battle.

## The trait drop

A win pays a **wildcard** — any trait in the game, not a fixed category. The
Arena needs three assembled Fighters to enter, so it is never allowed to hold a
piece a Fighter cannot be finished without. The same reasoning covers Realm
Raids and Boss Battles; see [[games-dhc-fighters]].

The roll is banded by **how far up you punched** — the gap between the rival
Crew's best Fighter and your own, by rarity score:

| Beating a Crew | Rolls on |
|---|---|
| better than yours | a better table, improving as the gap widens |
| far better than yours | the best table the Arena offers |
| weaker than yours | the standard table |

It is the same principle as raid looting: the risk you took is what is being
rewarded.

**One rewarded battle per opponent per day.** You can fight the same person
again, and it still counts on the ladder — it simply will not pay a second
trait. And the platform-wide cap of **three traits a day** applies here as
everywhere else, so the Arena cannot be farmed past it.

## The ladder

The monthly ladder is ranked on **wins**. Everyone has the same allowance of six
battles a day, so with equal attempts ranking on wins is ranking on win rate,
while still making an unspent battle a wasted one. Ties break on fewer losses,
then on the longest cascade chain you managed.

**250,000 CARBON** is split down the ladder by rank at the end of each month,
the same way every other monthly board pays — see [[platform-leaderboards]].

Only **attacking** results count on the ladder. Your Crew defends whether you
are there or not, and a board that paid for defensive results would pay for
being popular.

## Why it looks like it does

Battles are announced in Discord when they finish, and **the defender is tagged**
— an attack happens to a Crew whose owner did not choose the fight and was not
watching it, so they are the one who needs telling. The announcement is
illustrated with the **fiercest Fighter of the winning Crew**: the one that
actually dealt the most damage, not the one with the best stat line.

A battle is never resolved in your browser: your move is sent as "slide this gem there", the
server works out the whole exchange including the defending Crew's replies, and
what comes back is the result plus a list of what happened for the page to
animate. Nothing the browser says can change a number.

Every battle also keeps its seed and its move list, so any result can be
replayed exactly — a disputed outcome is checkable and a bug is reproducible.
