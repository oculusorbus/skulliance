# DHC Bosses

Three bosses stand up every **Thursday at 4pm CT** with a health bar each, and **everybody
shares them**. You can send **three Crews a round**, a round being 24 hours from 4pm CT; every
point of damage anyone lands comes off the same three bars, all week.

Your damage lands the moment you deal it. The bar everyone is looking at is current to
the last move anybody made, so nothing you land can be taken back by somebody else
finishing first, and the bosses on your own board are the shared crew: if another Crew
takes one down while you are out there, it goes down in front of you and the log says
so. If they take the last one down, your run ends there and everything you landed
counts in full.

It has **its own page**, the Bosses tab in the DHC strip, with its own Crew picker and
its own ladder. It is the same engine as [[games-dhc-arena]] underneath, which is why the
board, the gems, the reach rule and the bombs are identical, but the two are separate
games in every way that affects you: a separate daily allowance, a separate ladder, and
most importantly **a separate recovery clock**. A Fighter resting from a ranked Arena
battle can still be sent at the bosses, and the other way round.

## The crew

| | |
|---|---|
| **D34THBRNGR** | Three bodies, one wall. The most health, the slowest hands. |
| **CIP-666** | The bruiser. Hits back hardest of the three. |
| **X-K4T** | Fast and vicious. Falls soonest, hurts most getting there. |

They fight on **The Dark Blast**, the ground D34THBRNGR brings with it, where every
attack lands 10% harder. The arena is read off the front-rank opponent's background
exactly as it is in a ranked battle.

They are the same Fighters you fight in the Arena, with much larger numbers.
Same board, same matching, same reach, same bombs. What is different is that
there are three of them at once and their health does not reset when your run
ends.

## They get back up

**The crew is meant to fall mid-week.** Its health is scaled to a share of what
the week can deliver, so a community that turns up drops it around day four or
five. Surviving until Thursday is the unusual outcome, not the normal one.

That scaling is measured against **what runs actually deal**, not against a
theoretical best: once enough Crews have been sent, the crew's health is sized
from the real average this community produces. A sharper week gets a bigger
crew next time, a quieter one gets a smaller crew, and nobody tunes anything by
hand.

So a downed crew **stands back up the same week**, one wave higher, in a
different colourway, hitting harder. The week's question is not "can we drop
them", it is **how many times**.

Two things are true from the moment the first crew falls:

- **The best trait table is locked in** for everyone who takes part, and nothing
  later can take it back. A fresh crew at full health does not undo what the
  week already achieved.
- **Each wave is sized to the days that are left**, not to the whole week, so a
  crew that stands up on Tuesday can still be dropped by Thursday. Waves arrive
  faster as the week runs down.

Later waves are **angrier, not fatter**. Health sets the pace; power sets the
threat, so a wave 3 crew ends your runs sooner and costs you more Fighters for
every point of damage.

The whole crew respawns together, not boss by boss. A boss you drop stays down
until all three are down, which is what makes the panel readable as progress,
and what makes the moment they all fall worth something.

## Who else went in

Under the panels, the Crews sent this week: who sent them, the three Fighters
they fielded, the damage, and whether anybody walked away. The Fighters are
drawn rather than listed, and a Fighter who fell is greyed while a survivor
keeps its colour and a lit edge. Almost every run is a wipe, so the survivor is
the thing worth looking for.

## The cache: a trait for every round you turn up

Every **round** you send a Crew that does real damage banks **one trait**. Miss a
round and you finish the week one short.

**Rounds are worth more once the week has put a crew down.** A round fought while
wave 1 is standing banks one trait, wave 2 banks two, wave 3 banks three, and that
is where it stops. The multiplier is per **round**, not per Crew, so three Crews in
one sitting still bank that round once and the only way to a full week is still
turning up seven times.

That is deliberate, and it is the only thing later waves add. The trait **table**
is already at its best the moment the first crew falls, and there is nothing above
it anywhere on the platform, so what dropping a second and third crew buys the
community is **more rolls**, not better ones. The boxes on the page carry their own
multiplier so you can see what a round is worth before you spend it.

A round is **24 hours from 4pm CT**, the same boundary the week turns on — not a
calendar day. The page carries a live countdown to the next one, so you never have
to work out where the boundary is. So your three Crews reset at 4pm, not at midnight, and the Arena's
six a day (which really is a calendar day) resets at a different time. If you
send three Crews at two in the afternoon, you get three more at four.

Three Crews a day is deliberately too few to bank the week in one sitting, so
a full cache is seven separate visits. You can see it filling on the Bosses
page: seven boxes, numbered 1 to 7, with the current one outlined and any you
missed dotted out.

The boxes are **numbered rather than named** because the week turns at 4pm, so
a day here runs 4pm to 4pm and straddles two dates. "Day 3" is true wherever
you are; "Saturday" would not be.

The whole cache rolls when the week closes, on the table the community earned.

## Losing is the normal outcome, and it still pays

A boss has far more health than one Crew can remove, so most runs end with
your Fighters down and the bar a little lower. **That is the feature working.**
The day's reward is decided by damage, not by kills, so a run that got nowhere
near a kill still counts for exactly what it dealt.

## What the day pays

Which table your cache rolls on depends on how far **the whole community**
pushed the crew over the week:

| Crew damage | Table |
|---|---|
| under 25% | standard |
| 25% or more | improved |
| 50% or more | rare |
| **all three down** | **best** |

Every table keeps a chance of every rarity, including mythic. Nothing is
guaranteed; what improves is the odds.

The best table is **not** the top of that gradient. At 99% with one boss on a
sliver you are still one table short, which is the whole point of the last
push.

Because the reward rises continuously with damage, there is never a moment
when stopping is worth more than carrying on.

**The best table is the ceiling, and dropping one crew reaches it.** It is the
same table first place on every weekly board here gets, and nothing outranks it.
That is why the second and third crews of a week pay in **quantity** instead:
once the odds cannot improve, what is left to give is more rolls. See the cache
above.

## Your Fighters rest separately

A Fighter that fights a boss is unavailable for **bosses** for a few hours,
and longer if it fell. It is not benched from the Arena, and an Arena Crew
is not benched from the bosses. The two rosters are the same Fighters with
two independent cooldowns, so taking on the boss crew never costs you a
ranked battle.

## How big they are

The crew is sized every Thursday from how much firepower the platform could
actually field over a week: how many players have been active, how many Crews
they could send between them, and how much damage a run is worth. It is locked
when the crew stands up, so nobody who joins later makes it harder for the
people already fighting.

A quiet week gets a smaller crew. A busy one gets a bigger crew and the same
chance of clearing it. It is never sized beyond what the week can physically
deliver, so a perfect week always clears it.

## The Siege map

The ladder says where you stand. **Siege** says how the week is going, and who
is carrying it — which is the question a shared bar invites and a per-player
board cannot answer.

It has **its own tab** in the DHC strip, next to Rivalries. The boss crew sits
in the middle and everyone who sent a Crew at it is ringed around the outside,
with the Fighters they fielded drawn as portraits.

**The rings in the middle are this week's waves**, wave 1 outermost and the one
still standing at the centre with the bosses inside it. The **band between two
rings** is a wave, and its area is the health that wave was given — which makes
the picture the real shape of the week rather than decoration: wave 1 owns the
huge outer band because it stands for most of the week, and every later band is
thinner exactly as fast as that wave gets cheaper to drop — see **They get back up** above on how a wave is budgeted
against the days that are left. The crew still standing is the solid ring with
the portraits inside it; the waves already down are dashed.

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| A bigger circle | That player put more damage into the bar |
| A bigger portrait | That Fighter dealt more of it |
| A thicker line | More damage down that line |
| A longer line | They were here earlier — it reaches as far in as the waves they fought |
| An amber line and ring | They landed a last blow on a boss |
| An amber dot on a ring | Which boss, and on which wave |
| A dashed, faded portrait | A Fighter that has since been disassembled |

**Hover a player to isolate their line**; everything else fades and theirs starts
marching inward, the direction the damage went. **Click a player** to open their
week — Crews sent, rounds turned up, waves fought, and every Fighter they
fielded. **Click a Fighter** to open it in the same panel the Collection uses.

**A Fighter you have disassembled still appears**, dashed and unnamed. Taking one
apart deletes it, but the damage it dealt that week was real and stays in your
total, so the portrait stays too rather than quietly going missing and leaving
the picture disagreeing with the number.

**A line's length is when you turned up, not how well you did.** A player's line
reaches inward as far as the earliest wave they fought, so somebody who was
there from Thursday crosses every ring and somebody who arrived on Wednesday has
a short one near the middle. That is deliberately the only thing the wave you
reached says about you: wave 1 stands for roughly four of the week's seven days,
so "got to wave 5" means "played late in the week", not "is strong". Nothing on
the map sizes or ranks anyone by it.

**Circles are sized on total damage; the ladder ranks on damage per Fighter.**
Those are different numbers on purpose. Contribution to a shared bar really is
raw damage, so that is what a circle carries — but the ladder deliberately
divides by Fighters fielded so that a large roster cannot win on volume alone.
The biggest circle on the map is therefore not always the top row of the board.
Both figures are on every label.

**Three windows: this week, 4 weeks and 13 weeks.** They are whole weeks rather
than rolling days, because a boss crew belongs to exactly one week and the week
turns at 4pm Thursday — a rolling seven days would straddle that and show damage
against a crew that no longer exists. Over more than one week the middle shows the boss
crew with a bar for each week under it — how deep that week got — instead of one
week's waves, since wave numbers restart every Thursday.

The map is **public**, like the Collection and Rivalries: every name and avatar
on it is already on the weekly ladder.

## The weekly ladder

Everybody who sends a Crew appears. The minimum decides who can hold a **position** and who is paid, not who is listed, so if you are a Crew or two short the board tells you how many rather than leaving you off it.

A Crew that was still out there when the boss crew fell keeps every point it landed, because damage is committed as it is dealt rather than at the end of a run. It does not land the last blow and it is not credited with the kill, but the damage, the round and the trait are all yours.

Ranked on **damage per Fighter deployed**, not on total damage. Sending more
Fighters is not the same as fighting well, and a player with five good
Fighters should be able to top it. Total damage, Crews sent and Fighters sent
are all shown; none of them decide the order.

CARBON is paid down the board by rank when the week closes on Thursday, the same harmonic
split every board uses.

There is **no all-time board** here, and that is deliberate: the ranking is damage per
Fighter within one week, and adding that up across every week ever would put a player who
sent one perfect Crew in March above somebody who has turned up every day since. The week
is the season.

## If nobody finishes them

A new crew stands up on Thursday at 4pm at full health, back to wave 1. Nothing
carries over, and nothing is lost: everybody who took part is paid their cache
either way, just on a lower table.
